1Getting in

Create your login

Your account already exists — the office created it. You are not signing up, you are setting a password on an account that is already waiting for you. This takes about ninety seconds.

Go to protecthealth.com/appBookmark it. That is the only address you ever need. It works on your phone the same as your laptop.
  1. Email address — this is where you will sign in from now on. Use your ProtectHealth work email.
  2. Password — you do not have one yet, so skip this box today.
  3. Sign in — this is the button you will use every day after today.
  4. "Set up your login" — click this one today. It is the small link under the blue button.
  1. Work email — exactly the address the office has on file for you. If you have two, use the ProtectHealth one.
  2. Setup code — type 332633. Spaces and capitals do not matter.
  3. Create a password — at least 10 characters. It cannot contain your email address, and it cannot contain obvious words like "password" or "protecthealth".
  4. Confirm password — type the same thing again.
  5. Create my login — you are signed in immediately. No email, no waiting, no link to click.
Do not use "Reset password" todayThat path emails you a link and waits. Sixty of us are doing this at once on the same wifi. Use the setup code — it sends nothing and it works instantly.
Every day after todayEmail + password on the first screen. The setup code is a one-time thing and you will never need it again.
2Getting in

Finish your profile

This form opens by itself the first time you sign in. It is not paperwork. It is the thing that builds your public landing page and your booking calendar, and it puts you on the Meet the Brokers page of the website. Skip a field and those pages do not get built.

  1. Headshot — required. Clear, front-facing, JPG or PNG up to 8 MB. A phone selfie against a plain wall is fine. You can retake it any time. No photo means no landing page gets built at all.
  2. Phone number — required. This is the number printed on your landing page, your booking page, and the bottom of every email the system sends for you. Use the line you actually answer. Leave it blank and clients get the main office line instead of you.
  3. Contact email — optional. Leave it blank and we use your login address. Set it only if you want clients writing somewhere else. Changing it never affects how you sign in.
The bio is the one people skip, and it is the one that costs you the pageTwo or three sentences a prospect will actually read. Who you help, and how. Aim for at least a full sentence — the form will hold you there. This is the single most common reason a broker walks away thinking they are live when nothing was built.

A bio you can copy and edit

"I help self-employed Nevadans and 1099 contractors find real PPO coverage that fits how they actually work. Before I quote anything I want to understand your situation — your doctors, your budget, and what you are protecting. Straight answers, no pressure, and I will tell you when the answer is 'stay where you are.'"

  1. Lines of insurance you write — check every line you actually sell. This decides which content sections appear on your landing page. A Medicare-only writer will not show a health-only page. Check nothing and your page shows generic copy.
  2. Save profile — done. Your pages start building immediately.
You can change all of this laterBottom-left of the sidebar, Edit profile. Every change rebuilds your public pages within a few minutes.

4Getting in

Set your availability

Do this on Monday. Until you do, you are on the default Monday to Friday, 9 to 5, and anyone can book you at 9am on a day you do not work.

  1. Turn days on and off. Unchecking a day removes it from your booking page entirely — nobody can pick it.
  2. Set your real hours for each day. Times are Las Vegas time. Your out-of-state clients see their own timezone automatically, so do not do the math yourself.
  3. Saturday and Sunday are off by default. If you genuinely work weekends, turn them on. If you do not, leave them alone.
  4. Booking settings — how long each appointment is, how far ahead people can book, and how much notice you need. The minimum-notice setting is the one that stops someone booking you in fifteen minutes.
Two minutes now saves you a month of awkward callsNine of us have no phone number on file and none of us has set hours yet. Set it while you are in the room.
5Getting in

Connect your Google Calendar

This is the one that prevents double-bookings. Once linked, any time you are already busy on Google is automatically blocked on your ProtectHealth booking page. A client physically cannot book on top of your dentist appointment or a listing showing.

  1. Sidebar → Availability, then scroll to the bottom card, Link your Google Calendar.
  2. Click Connect Google Calendar and pick the Google account where your real appointments live.
  3. Google says "Google hasn't verified this app." That is expected and it is safe — our CRM is a private company tool, not a public app in the Google store. Click Advanced, then Go to ProtectHealth (unsafe). Nothing about it is actually unsafe; that is just the wording Google uses for private company apps.
  4. Leave both calendar boxes checked and click Continue. You land back on a green Connected badge.
  5. Tick every calendar that means you are genuinely busy. Leave holiday and birthday calendars unchecked — they will block real client bookings if you check them. Then Save calendar selection.
What we can and cannot seeWe read the times you are busy so your booking page can block them. Clients only ever see open or unavailable time, never what you are doing. You can disconnect any time from the same screen.
Test it in sixty secondsMake an event in Google for tomorrow at 2pm called "Test." Go back to Availability and click Preview my booking page. The 2pm slot should be gone. Delete the test event and it comes back on its own.

If the slot did not disappear: the event is probably marked Free in Google rather than Busy. Google treats Free as available, so we do too.

7Using it

Adding a client

Everything starts as a contact. Your contacts are yours — no other broker can see them, and that was verified at the database level, not just hidden in the interface.

  1. + Add Contact — the main way you put someone in the system.
  2. Filters — narrow by disposition, tag, line, carrier, premium, or birthday month.
  3. Columns — tick what you want to see. Your choice is remembered.
  1. First name is the only required field. Everything else can come later — add what you have and keep moving.
  2. Disposition — where this person stands right now. This is the most important field on the form. See the next section.
Check a line of insurance or the deal never appearsEach line you tick creates a deal on that line's pipeline board. A contact saved with no line checked is invisible on the Pipeline tab. If someone goes missing from your board, this is why. Open the record and add the line — it shows up straight away.

You can also add carriers, dependents, a date of birth (which drives the birthday reminders on your dashboard), and free-text notes. Add what you know today, fill the rest in as you learn it.

8Using it

Dispositions — and the one rule that matters

The disposition is how the system knows what is happening with a person and what to send them. It is a dropdown on every contact record. Change it as the relationship changes.

✅ Active Client — required on every client you write
The moment you set this, three things happen automatically: the client is marked in-force, you get the commission-qualified email, and your client gets the review request with the $100 gift-card drawing. This is not optional. If you wrote the policy, the disposition is Active Client. No exceptions — this is how you get paid credit and how the review goes out.
Lead
The default. A new person you have not worked yet.
Follow-Up
Live conversation, not closed. Your working list.
No Answer
Tried, could not reach them. Try again.
Not Interested
Real answer, and it was no. Keeps your board honest.
Wrong Number
Bad data, not a bad lead.
Do Not Call
They asked. Respect it and mark it.
Why "every client must be Active Client" is a hard ruleNothing downstream fires without it. No commission notice to you. No review request to your client. No in-force record. A written policy sitting on "Follow-Up" is invisible to the whole system — it looks like you did not write it.
You will see confirmationSet it and a message appears: "Marked Active Client — client is now in-force." That is your receipt that it went through.
9Using it

Working your pipeline

One board per line of insurance. A deal is created for you automatically the moment a contact has a line checked — you never create a deal by hand.

  1. Drag a card sideways to move a deal through the stages: New Lead → Appointment Set → Contacted → Needs Analysis → Quoted → Application Submitted, and on to written.
  2. The dropdown at the top switches boards. The same person can be on your Health board and your Life board at once — those are two separate deals, and that is deliberate.

Each card shows how long it has been sitting in its current stage. A card that has not moved in a week is telling you something.

11Using it

Reviews

You do not send review requests. Marking a client Active Client does it for you, the same second. This tab is where you watch what happened.

  1. Your four numbers — requests sent, how many opened the link, how many went to Google with 4 or 5 stars, and how many need follow-up.
  2. Every request — the full list: who was asked, who opened it, who rated, and exactly what they wrote.
One honest limitationFour and five stars go straight to Google, so we can see that someone was sent there but not whether they finished posting — Google does not report that back. One to three stars never reach Google at all.
12Reference

House rules

  1. Every client you write gets marked Active Client. Not optional. It is how you get commission credit and how the review request goes out.
  2. Every contact gets at least one line of insurance, or it never appears on your pipeline.
  3. Keep your disposition current. A stale board is worse than an empty one.
  4. Set your availability and connect Google Calendar before you share your booking link with anyone.
  5. Open a lead inside the CRM to claim it. Unclaimed leads reassign after an hour during business hours. There is no app to download — opening it here is the claim.
  6. Use Get Support, not a group text. It creates a real ticket that does not get lost.

Glossary — what is this button for?

Bookmark this section. When you cannot remember what something does, it is here.

Contact
Any person in your book. A lead, a prospect, or a client — they are all contacts, distinguished by their disposition.
Disposition
Where a person stands right now. Drives what the system sends. Active Client is the one that triggers commission and review emails.
Line of insurance
Health, Medicare, Life, Dental, Vision, Annuities, Group Benefits, Supplements, Accident/Gap. Ticking one creates a deal on that pipeline.
Deal
One opportunity on one line for one person. Created automatically. Someone buying health and life is two deals.
Stage
A column on the pipeline board. Drag a card sideways to change it.
In-force
The policy is active and the client is really yours. Set by marking Active Client.
Landing page
Your public page at /broker/your-name. Photo, bio, your lines, booking built in.
Booking page
Your calendar at /book/your-name. One job: someone picks a time.
Availability
The hours you allow bookings. Different from your Google Calendar, which blocks time on top of it.
Minimum notice
How far ahead someone must book. Stops a stranger taking a slot fifteen minutes from now.
Review request
The automatic email with the $100 gift-card drawing, sent to a client the second you mark them Active Client.
Claiming a lead
Opening a new lead inside the CRM. Unopened leads reassign after an hour during business hours.
Tag
A free-form label for filtering — "referral", "hot lead", "follow-up". Use them however helps you.
Carrier
The insurance company on a policy. 38 are loaded; pick from the list so reporting stays clean.
Ask Pete
The internal AI resource centre. Carrier and plan questions, answered from our own documents.
Get Support
Opens a ticket with the office, prefilled with who you are.
