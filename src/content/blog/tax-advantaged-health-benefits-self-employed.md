"Tax-advantaged health benefits" gets used as though it names one thing. It names four, and those four do not stack the way most people assume. Two of them actively cancel each other out.

That last sentence is the reason this page exists. A self-employed Nevadan can build an arrangement that looks like an obvious win, then lose a deduction worth more than the arrangement ever saved. The discovery usually arrives in April, from an accountant, in a tone nobody enjoys.

Here is the comparison drawn honestly: what each structure does, who genuinely qualifies, what quietly disqualifies, and where the interactions bite.

One thing up front. We are insurance nerds, not tax professionals, and this page is tax-heavy by nature. Treat every line below as a map for a conversation with a licensed tax professional, not as filing advice.

## What are the four tax-advantaged health structures for the self-employed?

Four structures exist: the self-employed health insurance deduction, Section 105 medical reimbursement plans, QSEHRA, and ICHRA. Only the first requires no payroll at all.

### The self-employed health insurance deduction

The baseline, and the only structure on this list available to a Las Vegas freelancer working alone. Premiums for medical, dental, vision and qualified long-term care coverage come off income on Schedule 1 of Form 1040, line 17, for the taxpayer, a spouse, dependents, and a child who is under 27 at year end even when that child is not claimed as a dependent.

No plan document. No employees. No third-party administrator. Just correct filing, which in certain cases means attaching Form 7206.

### Section 105 medical reimbursement plans

A Section 105 arrangement lets a business reimburse medical expenses tax-free rather than reimbursing premiums alone. The classic self-employed version runs through a legitimately employed spouse who receives family medical coverage as an employee benefit.

Powerful where it genuinely fits. Document-heavy always. The mechanics sit in [what is a Section 105 plan](/qa/what-is-a-section-105-plan), and the employment question that gates it sits in [can hiring a spouse unlock health benefits](/qa/can-hiring-a-spouse-unlock-health-benefits).

### QSEHRA

A qualified small employer health reimbursement arrangement, built for employers with fewer than 50 employees that offer no group health plan. Annual reimbursement caps are set by the IRS. Administration is far lighter than a group plan, which makes a QSEHRA a natural first benefit for a small Clark County trades business that has never offered anything at all. Per [HealthCare.gov's small employer guidance](https://www.healthcare.gov/small-businesses/learn-more/qsehra/), a group health plan cannot run alongside it, and written notice is due to eligible employees at least 90 days before the plan year starts.

### ICHRA

No size cap, no contribution cap, and allowances that can vary by legitimate employee class. ICHRA was created by a [final rule issued jointly by the Treasury, Labor, and Health and Human Services departments](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2019/06/20/2019-12571/health-reimbursement-arrangements-and-other-account-based-group-health-plans) on June 20, 2019, became available January 1, 2020, and operates under regulations at 26 CFR 54.9802-4, 29 CFR 2590.702-2 and 45 CFR 146.123. Full mechanics live in [the ICHRA guide](/blog-post/what-is-an-ichra).

## Who qualifies for each structure, and what disqualifies a business?

Payroll decides three of the four. A business whose only worker is the owner qualifies for the deduction and for nothing else described on this page.

| Structure | Who qualifies | What disqualifies |
|---|---|---|
| Self-employed health insurance deduction | Any self-employed taxpayer with net self-employment income | Eligibility for a subsidized plan through the taxpayer's own or a spouse's employer in a given month; net self-employment income too low; QSEHRA participation |
| Section 105 medical reimbursement plan | A business with a legitimate W-2 employee, classically an employed spouse | No genuine employment relationship, no written plan document, no substantiation; owner-only businesses |
| QSEHRA | Employer with fewer than 50 employees offering no group health plan | Maintaining a group health plan; owner-only businesses; the arrangement itself blocks the self-employed deduction |
| ICHRA | Employer of any size with at least one employee who is not an owner or that owner's spouse | Solo operators; more-than-2-percent S corporation shareholders participating personally; premium tax credits are forfeited on accepted coverage |

Read the right-hand column twice. Eligibility is rarely where people go wrong. Disqualifiers are.

## How does the self-employed health insurance deduction work?

Premiums come off income above the line on Schedule 1 of Form 1040, line 17, which means the benefit lands whether or not the household itemizes. That single feature makes the deduction the workhorse of this entire category.

Coverage that counts includes medical, dental, vision and qualified long-term care policies for the taxpayer, a spouse, dependents, and a child under 27 at year end.

### Two limits that catch people every year

The first limit is employer eligibility. The deduction is unavailable for any month the taxpayer was **eligible to participate** in a subsidized health plan through their own employer or a spouse's employer. Eligibility, not enrollment. A Realtor who declined her husband's plan still lost the deduction for those months, because the offer existed. The [Form 7206 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i7206) spell out the month-by-month test.

The second limit is income. The deduction cannot exceed net self-employment income from the business under which the plan is established. A convention contractor with a thin year deducts less than the premiums actually paid, regardless of what was written in checks.

### When Form 7206 enters the picture

Form 7206 becomes necessary in specific situations rather than universally: more than one source of self-employment income, a Form 2555 filer, or long-term care premium amounts in the calculation. Plenty of straightforward returns never touch it. The deeper walkthrough sits in [is self-employed health insurance tax deductible](/qa/is-self-employed-health-insurance-tax-deductible) and, in Nevada terms specifically, in [can self-employed Nevadans deduct health insurance premiums](/qa/can-self-employed-nevadans-deduct-health-insurance-premiums).

Again: we are insurance nerds, not tax professionals. The eligibility test above is exactly where a licensed tax professional earns the fee.

## Why does a QSEHRA cancel the self-employed health insurance deduction?

QSEHRA coverage counts as participation in a subsidized health plan. That classification blocks the self-employed health insurance deduction under section 162(l), so the same premium dollars cannot do both jobs.

This is the interaction that costs real money, and it almost never appears in the sales pitch for a reimbursement arrangement. The pitch describes tax-free reimbursement. The pitch does not describe the above-the-line deduction being switched off in the same breath.

Sequence matters. A Las Vegas owner who sets up a QSEHRA for two employees and then folds in a spouse through some creative reading of the rules has usually created two problems rather than one.

The correct move is boring and effective. Price the deduction. Price the arrangement. Compare the two figures for the household and for the staff separately, because the answer for the crew and the answer for the owner are frequently different. The employee side of that decision is covered in [the small business health insurance guide](/blog-post/small-business-health-insurance).

## Can a solo operator in Las Vegas set up an ICHRA?

No. An ICHRA requires at least one employee who is not a self-employed owner or that owner's spouse, which shuts out every solo operator regardless of revenue, entity type or how the business looks on paper.

This disqualifier surprises more people than anything else on this page, because ICHRA marketing rarely leads with it. A Realtor closing thirty transactions a year, a convention services contractor billing six figures, a freelance producer with steady Strip clients: none can reimburse their own premiums through an ICHRA while working alone. The alternatives are mapped in [health insurance options for self-employed Nevadans](/blog-post/health-insurance-options-self-employed-nevada) and, for the gig side of the valley, in [health insurance for freelancers and gig workers](/blog-post/health-insurance-for-freelancers-and-gig-workers).

### The S corporation wrinkle

An S corporation shareholder owning more than 2 percent of the stock is treated like a partner rather than an employee for fringe benefit purposes, per [IRS Publication 15-B](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p15b). Premiums generally run through payroll and land on the W-2. That treatment does not stop the business from offering an ICHRA to its staff. It stops the owner from participating personally in most cases, which is a different sentence entirely and gets confused constantly. The detail sits in [can an S-corp owner use an ICHRA](/qa/can-an-s-corp-owner-use-an-ichra).

### The subsidy trade

Accepting an ICHRA forecloses premium tax credits for that coverage. For a household currently receiving a substantial advance credit through Nevada Health Link, an allowance can be a downgrade dressed as a raise. That comparison is the whole subject of [ICHRA versus marketplace health insurance](/blog-post/ichra-vs-marketplace-health-insurance).

## What does a Section 105 plan require in practice?

A Section 105 plan requires a real employment relationship, real wages for real work, a written plan document, and substantiated reimbursements. The requirements are the point, not paperwork friction to be minimized.

The classic self-employed setup employs a spouse in the business and provides family medical coverage as an employee benefit, with the owner covered as the employee's family member. Where the employment is genuine and documented, the arrangement can reimburse a broader set of medical costs than premiums alone.

Where the employment is a fiction invented in December, the arrangement is an audit exposure with a tax-planning label on it. Timesheets, job duties, wages consistent with the work performed, and payroll filings all have to exist, because the work actually happened.

This structure sits further into tax territory than anything else on this page, which is why we say it plainly a third time: we are insurance nerds, not tax professionals. A Section 105 conversation without a licensed tax professional in it is a sales conversation.

## What do the numbers look like for a Las Vegas Realtor?

An illustrative sketch makes the interaction concrete. Every figure below is invented for demonstration and is not a quoted rate, since real numbers depend on age, zip code, plan selection and household composition.

Picture a Henderson Realtor, married, filing jointly. Net self-employment income lands at $96,000 for the year. Household health premiums run $780 a month, or $9,360 annually. Her husband works for a Clark County employer that offers coverage, and she declined it.

Path one, the deduction alone. Nothing is deductible for the months a subsidized employer plan was available to her through her husband, and in this sketch that offer stood all twelve months. The deduction she assumed was automatic is worth zero. That is the whole illustration. Nothing was structured wrong, and she still lost every dollar of it by declining a plan rather than never being offered one.

Now change one fact. Suppose the husband is a 1099 event contractor with no employer offer at all. The same $9,360 is now deductible against $96,000 of net self-employment income, subject to the income limit, which it clears comfortably. At an assumed 22 percent marginal federal rate, that is roughly $2,059 in federal tax not paid.

Then add the trap. Suppose she also brings on a part-time W-2 assistant and a sales rep suggests a QSEHRA as a first benefit. If the household ends up covered under that QSEHRA, the $9,360 deduction is blocked, and the arrangement has to be worth more than $2,059 in this sketch before the change is even neutral. The arrangement might be worth it for the two employees. It is a loss for the owner if nobody runs the comparison first.

Two structures, both legitimate, and a net worse outcome. That is what stacking without checking looks like.

## Why does Nevada change the value of a federal health deduction?

Nevada charges no state income tax, so the federal deduction is the entire tax benefit. A self-employed Nevadan cannot stack a state-level deduction on top of the federal one, because no state-level deduction exists to stack.

Residents of California or Oregon watch the same premium dollars reduce two tax bills. A Las Vegas hospitality contractor watches them reduce one. That does not make the federal deduction less valuable. It means every calculation stops at the federal line, and any planning article assuming state stacking is describing somebody else's state.

The calendar matters just as much. Open enrollment through [Nevada Health Link](https://www.nevadahealthlink.com/) runs November 1 through January 15, and a plan selected by December 31 starts January 1 while one selected between January 1 and January 15 starts February 1. Those dates bound the coverage decisions feeding all four structures. And before taking structure advice from anyone, including us, any Nevadan can verify a producer's license through the [Nevada Division of Insurance](https://doi.nv.gov/).

## Which structure should a self-employed Nevadan look at first?

Start with payroll, because payroll answers three of the four questions instantly. No employees means the deduction and marketplace coverage, and the conversation about arrangements is over before it starts.

With employees on the books, the order changes. Check whether a group plan already exists, since a QSEHRA cannot coexist with one. Check what the crew receives in premium tax credits today, since an ICHRA replaces those credits rather than adding to them. Check entity type, since a more-than-2-percent S corporation shareholder faces a different answer than a C corporation owner. The Realtor profile that generates most of these questions in Clark County is covered in [the Realtor health insurance guide](/blog-post/realtor-health-insurance-guide).

Then check the household separately from the business. The owner's answer and the staff's answer diverge more often than not, and a single blended recommendation hides whichever party the change would hurt.

The product should serve the strategy, not become the strategy. Every structure on this page is legitimate, federally recognized and useful in the right hands. What separates a good outcome from an expensive one is not which arrangement gets chosen. It is whether anyone checked what the chosen arrangement switches off.
