The pitch lands hard on freelancers because it is built for them: real-sounding coverage at a fraction of the price, available today, no window to wait for. The price is real. The coverage is where the asterisks live.

## What makes a short-term premium so much lower?

Subtraction, not efficiency. Short-term plans sit outside the rules that require comprehensive coverage, which lets them do four things a marketplace plan cannot.

They can exclude pre-existing conditions, often with claims reviewed against prior medical history. They can cap what gets paid, annually or over the life of the policy. They can skip entire categories of essential coverage, with maternity, mental health and prescription benefits the ones most commonly missing. And they can decline an applicant outright through medical underwriting.

None of that is hidden. It is the product working exactly as designed, and the design is a bridge rather than a house.

## When does a short-term plan genuinely make sense?

For a bounded gap with a date on the far end. A plan ending mid month before the next one starts, a wait for an enrollment window that has already been scheduled, a move in progress.

The tell of a legitimate use is that somebody can say out loud when the coverage ends and what replaces it. Thirty-one days between a W-2 job ending and a new plan starting is a real gap. Twelve months of driving because the marketplace felt expensive in November is not a gap, it is a decision that has been renamed.

Worth checking before buying anything: several of the events that create these gaps also open a special enrollment period for a real plan. Losing job-based coverage, marriage, a birth or adoption, and a permanent move into Nevada all qualify, and the list is worked through in [what is a qualifying life event](/qa/what-is-a-qualifying-life-event). A household that qualifies for a special enrollment period usually does not need the bridge at all.

## What does a short-term plan fail to do for a freelance household?

Four things, and each one lands harder on somebody whose income stops the day the work stops.

### No protection against the expensive year

The premium describes a normal year. A benefit cap describes the worst one, and the worst one is the only scenario coverage exists for. A hospitalization has no ceiling and a capped policy does, which is the whole exposure in one sentence.

### No premium tax credit

Short-term coverage earns no subsidy, so the comparison people make is usually between a short-term premium and a full-price marketplace premium. That is the wrong comparison. Credits are calculated on net self-employment income after mileage, equipment and other business expenses, which often narrows the gap far more than the sticker prices suggest. The method is in [how freelancers estimate income for health subsidies](/qa/how-do-freelancers-estimate-income-for-health-subsidies).

### No help with the pre-existing condition already in the file

A freelancer who has been managing anything, a thyroid prescription, a bad shoulder, an old back injury from loading event cases, is buying a policy that may specifically decline to pay for the one thing already known about.

### Nothing to build on

Years on short-term coverage means no primary care relationship, no baseline labs and a condition found late rather than managed early. That cost never shows up on a premium comparison and it is frequently the largest number in the whole decision.

## Can an employer reimbursement arrangement pay for a short-term plan?

No. Short-term coverage does not satisfy the integration requirement, so an individual coverage HRA allowance cannot be spent on one.

This catches people at exactly the wrong moment. A freelancer who takes a contract role at a business offering an allowance, or who has grown a business with W-2 staff, cannot direct that money toward a short-term policy. The arrangement requires an individual plan that meets the requirement, and the structure is explained in [what is an ICHRA](/blog-post/what-is-an-ichra). Fixed-indemnity and healthcare sharing arrangements fail the same test for the same reason.

## What should a Nevada freelancer do for year-round coverage instead?

Build on the marketplace. Nevada Health Link runs the individual market here, credits are calculated on profit rather than gross receipts, and the resulting price is frequently nothing like the number that made short-term coverage look attractive.

The full set of paths open to a 1099 earner in this state sits in [the freelancer and gig worker coverage guide](/blog-post/health-insurance-for-freelancers-and-gig-workers), and it includes options a short-term pitch never mentions: a spouse's employer plan, Nevada Medicaid during a lean stretch, and reimbursement arrangements once a business has real payroll.

Nevada Health Link publishes a [directory of certified brokers and navigators](https://nevadahealthlink.com/find-assistance/), and individual plans are priced identically whether a licensed broker is involved or not, so the comparison itself costs the household nothing.

## How should a short-term offer be vetted before anything gets signed?

Five checks, and the license check comes first. Nevada licenses the producers who sell these products, and confirming a license takes about a minute, well before a payment method changes hands.

Start at the [Nevada Division of Insurance](https://doi.nv.gov/), then read for the exclusion list rather than the brochure. Ask for the benefit caps in writing, annual and lifetime. Ask for the maximum term and what happens at renewal, because a renewal that requires new underwriting is a policy that can end the moment a claim makes it expensive. Ask whether the product is insurance at all, since sharing arrangements and per-event indemnity products are marketed alongside short-term plans and are not the same thing.

Anything sold with urgency deserves a slower week. Urgency is a sales technique, not a coverage feature.

## What does the Las Vegas calendar do to this decision?

It creates the exact conditions short-term plans are marketed into. Income here moves with the convention schedule, and the slow stretch between big shows is when a freelance household is most tempted to trade real coverage for a cheaper monthly line.

That is precisely backward. A slow stretch is when a lower income estimate may increase the premium tax credit, and when a household may qualify for Nevada Medicaid, which enrolls year round rather than only in the fall. The cheaper answer is usually inside the real system, not outside it. Anyone comparing options should also read [do gig workers get health insurance from Uber or DoorDash](/qa/do-gig-workers-get-health-insurance-from-uber-or-doordash), because the same drivers get both pitches.

We are insurance nerds, not tax professionals. Once deductions or business structure enter the conversation, a licensed tax professional belongs in it too.

The product should serve the strategy, not become the strategy. A short-term plan can serve a strategy that already has an end date written into it. [Book a conversation](/self-employed) before assuming that a stopgap is the only thing available.
