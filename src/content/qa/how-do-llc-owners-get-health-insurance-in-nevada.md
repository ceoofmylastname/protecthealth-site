"I formed an LLC, what does that get me for health insurance?" is one of the most-asked questions in self-employed coverage and one of the most misunderstood. The honest answer: the LLC is the wrapper, not the strategy. Filing with the Nevada Secretary of State changed the liability picture and the bank account. It did not change what a carrier will sell.

## What is the default path for a Nevada LLC owner?

An individual plan bought through Nevada Health Link, priced with premium tax credits on net business income, plus the self-employed premium deduction where eligible.

That combination is the workhorse for the large majority of LLC owners in Clark County, and it is the baseline against which every fancier structure should be measured. Nevada Health Link is the state-based exchange operated by the Silver State Health Insurance Exchange, so a Nevadan starting on the federal site gets redirected. Enrollment runs [November 1 through December 31](https://nevadahealthlink.com/get-help/when-to-enroll/), a window that lost roughly two weeks compared with what most people remember.

The subsidy math is where LLC owners regularly leave money behind. Premium tax credits are calculated on net self-employment income after business expenses, not on gross revenue. A single-member LLC invoicing $140,000 that legitimately deducts vehicle, equipment, contractor payments and home office costs may show a net figure in a completely different subsidy band. Owners assume the invoice total disqualifies the household and never run the numbers. Running them costs nothing.

## Why does the tax election matter more than the LLC?

Because benefits law reads the federal tax classification, not the filing made in Carson City. The same LLC can behave three different ways depending on that election.

### Taxed as a sole proprietorship

The default for a single-member LLC. Sole proprietor rules apply straight through: the deduction is claimed on Schedule 1 of Form 1040, line 17, and no group plan is available without a W-2 employee beyond the owner and spouse. The reasoning behind that wall is in [can a sole proprietor get group health insurance](/qa/can-a-sole-proprietor-get-group-health-insurance).

### Taxed as an S corporation

The election most often sold to Nevada LLC owners as a payroll tax play, and the one that most changes the health benefit picture. A shareholder owning more than 2 percent is treated like a partner rather than an employee for fringe benefit purposes, per IRS [Publication 15-B](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p15b).

Two consequences follow. Premiums generally have to be paid or reimbursed by the corporation and reported on the shareholder's W-2 before the personal deduction is claimed, so the paperwork sequence is not optional. And participation in the company's own reimbursement arrangement is generally off the table for that owner, which is the subject of [can an S corp owner use an ICHRA](/qa/can-an-s-corp-owner-use-an-ichra).

An owner who elects S corporation status for self-employment tax reasons, without anyone flagging the benefits consequence, can end up worse off overall. The election and the coverage plan belong in the same conversation.

### Taxed as a partnership

A multi-member LLC by default. Partners follow partner rules rather than employee rules, with their own sequence for getting premiums onto the return correctly.

Same three letters on the door. Three separate rulebooks behind it.

## When do group plans and reimbursement arrangements open up?

When the LLC has at least one W-2 common-law employee who is not an owner or an owner's spouse. Payroll is the trigger, not revenue and not entity type.

Once real staff exist, three doors unlock. Small-group coverage becomes purchasable. An [individual coverage HRA](https://www.healthcare.gov/small-businesses/learn-more/individual-coverage-hra/) becomes available as a way to reimburse employees for individual premiums tax free, explained in full in [what is an ICHRA](/blog-post/what-is-an-ichra). And [a QSEHRA](/qa/what-is-a-qsehra) becomes an option for employers under 50 employees who do not offer a group plan.

Owner participation in any of those still depends on the tax classification above. This is the point where a lot of Las Vegas businesses discover the arrangement they set up covers the crew beautifully and does nothing for the owner who paid for it.

## Does an LLC owner get the self-employed deduction?

Generally yes, on Schedule 1 of Form 1040, line 17, covering medical, dental, vision and qualified long-term care premiums for the owner, a spouse, dependents, and a child under age 27 at year end.

Two limits do the real work. The deduction cannot exceed net self-employment income, which bites in a startup year or a slow year. And it is unavailable for any month the owner was eligible to participate in a subsidized plan through an employer, including a spouse's employer. The full breakdown sits in [can self-employed Nevadans deduct health insurance premiums](/qa/can-self-employed-nevadans-deduct-health-insurance-premiums).

Nevada has no state income tax, so this deduction is a federal benefit and nothing more. There is no second layer waiting at the state level, which is worth knowing for owners who relocated a business here from a state that had one.

## What should a Nevada LLC owner check before open enrollment?

Four things, and none of them are about the entity.

**Net income for the coming year, not last year.** The credit runs on a forward estimate and gets reconciled at filing. For an LLC with lumpy commission or project income, this is the hardest number on the application and the one most worth getting right.

**Whether the household's doctors are in network for the new plan year.** Networks in the Las Vegas valley shift between plan years. A practice that was in network last January is not guaranteed to be in network next January, and checking takes minutes.

**Prescriptions and formulary tiers.** Two plans at the same premium can differ by hundreds of dollars a year on a single maintenance drug.

**Whether an S corporation election is coming.** If an accountant is recommending one for next year, the benefits consequence has to be priced now rather than discovered in April.
