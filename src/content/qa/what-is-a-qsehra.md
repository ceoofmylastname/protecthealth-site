QSEHRA is the older, smaller sibling in the reimbursement family. Capped, simpler, and for a Las Vegas shop offering benefits for the first time, sometimes exactly the right size.

## What is a QSEHRA in plain terms?

A Qualified Small Employer Health Reimbursement Arrangement. A qualifying small business sets an allowance, employees buy their own individual coverage, and the business reimburses them tax free up to annual limits that are adjusted each year.

Nothing about it is a group plan. There is no roster, no single network everybody shares, no renewal negotiation over a pooled rate. Each employee holds a policy in their own name, and the business contributes a defined dollar amount toward it. The federal overview sits on the [healthcare.gov QSEHRA page for small businesses](https://www.healthcare.gov/small-businesses/learn-more/qsehra/), and the employee-side version is at [healthcare.gov job-based help for QSEHRA](https://www.healthcare.gov/job-based-help/qsehra/).

That structure is why the arrangement travels well in a city like this one. A five person business with staff spread from Summerlin to Henderson is not trying to find one network that works for everybody. Each person solves for their own doctors.

## Which businesses can offer a QSEHRA?

Two gates, and both have to hold. Fewer than 50 employees, and no group health plan offered alongside the arrangement.

### The size gate

Under 50 is the ceiling. A business that crosses it has outgrown this structure and needs to look at an individual coverage HRA or a group plan instead.

### The no-group-plan gate

A QSEHRA cannot sit next to a group health plan. This is not a nuance to work around; it is a condition of the arrangement, and a business that wants to keep a group plan for one part of the team and reimburse another part is describing an ICHRA rather than a QSEHRA.

### The employee gate

The one that eliminates most people who ask about it. A QSEHRA requires at least one employee who is not a self-employed owner or that owner's spouse. A solo operator cannot stand one up to reimburse personal premiums, and neither can a two-person business consisting of an owner and that owner's spouse.

That rule catches a lot of Nevada freelancers who were told otherwise, and the wider explanation of why sits in [the freelancer and gig worker coverage guide](/blog-post/health-insurance-for-freelancers-and-gig-workers).

## What has to happen before the plan year starts?

The annual notice. Eligible employees have to receive it at least 90 days before the plan year begins, which puts the deadline in the fall for a business running on a calendar year.

The notice is not a formality. It tells employees what the allowance is and warns that the arrangement affects premium tax credit eligibility, which is information an employee needs before open enrollment rather than after. A business that decides on a QSEHRA in mid December has already missed the window for a January start, and the honest answer at that point is to plan properly for the following year.

## What does an employee have to do to keep the reimbursement tax free?

Hold minimum essential coverage and prove it. Reimbursements are tax free to the employee only while qualifying coverage is in force, which makes substantiation part of the arrangement rather than paperwork bolted onto it.

That condition also rules out the products marketed hardest to people who have never had benefits before. Short-term plans, fixed-indemnity policies and healthcare sharing arrangements are not the coverage this rule contemplates. An employee who buys one and submits it for reimbursement has a problem that surfaces at tax time rather than at the moment of purchase.

## Why does a QSEHRA block the self-employed health insurance deduction?

Because it counts as participation in a subsidized health plan. Coverage under a QSEHRA makes the section 162(l) self-employed deduction unavailable for the months involved.

Owners rarely see this coming, and it is the point where an insurance decision quietly becomes a tax decision. The deduction rules are laid out in [can freelancers write off health insurance premiums](/qa/can-freelancers-write-off-health-insurance-premiums), and there is a related structural limit worth knowing: shareholders holding more than 2 percent of an S corporation may not participate in a QSEHRA at all. The S corporation angle is covered in [can an S corp owner use an ICHRA](/qa/can-an-s-corp-owner-use-an-ichra).

We are insurance nerds, not tax professionals. Entity structure, ownership attribution and deduction eligibility are exactly where a licensed tax professional belongs in the room, before anything gets adopted rather than after.

## How does a QSEHRA differ from an ICHRA?

Three axes decide it: size, caps and flexibility.

| Feature | QSEHRA | ICHRA |
|---|---|---|
| Employer size | Fewer than 50 employees | Any size |
| Contribution limits | Annual caps, adjusted each year | No federal contribution cap |
| Employee classes | Generally uniform terms | Allowances by legitimate class |
| Group plan alongside | Not permitted | Permitted for different classes |

A business that wants to contribute more than the caps allow, or to offer different allowances to full-time and part-time staff, has outgrown the QSEHRA and lands on an individual coverage HRA. The federal summary of that structure sits at [healthcare.gov on the individual coverage HRA](https://www.healthcare.gov/small-businesses/learn-more/individual-coverage-hra/), and the head to head is worked through in [what is an ICHRA](/blog-post/what-is-an-ichra).

Both arrangements share the same employee requirement, so neither one rescues a business that has no employees beyond the owner.

## What does a QSEHRA look like for a Las Vegas small business?

A defined line in the budget instead of a renewal negotiation. That is the practical appeal for an owner who has never offered benefits and is not looking to start managing a group plan.

Picture a nine person operation servicing event and hospitality clients across the valley, staff living from the northwest to Henderson, ages spread across three decades. A group plan asks that owner to pick one network for everybody. A QSEHRA asks for a number, and lets each employee solve for the doctors they already use. Any dollar figure quoted for that allowance would be illustrative rather than a rate, since the caps are set federally and adjusted annually.
