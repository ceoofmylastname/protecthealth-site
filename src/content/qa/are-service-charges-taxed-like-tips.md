The receipt might say gratuity. The classification does not follow the label on the receipt, it follows whether the customer had a choice.

## Are mandatory service charges treated as tips for tax purposes?

No. A mandatory service charge is treated as regular wages rather than as a tip. The character of the payment changes the moment payment stops being optional, and everything downstream changes with it.

A tip has three properties. The customer decides freely whether to pay. The customer sets the amount. The customer chooses who receives the money. Remove any one of those and the payment is no longer a tip in the federal sense.

A banquet contract with a mandatory percentage attached fails all three tests at once. So does an auto-gratuity on a party of eight, a required event staffing charge, or a compulsory delivery fee. The money is revenue to the business, and whatever share is passed through to employees arrives as ordinary wages, exactly like hourly pay. The IRS covers the treatment of tips and the employer duties that surround them in its guidance on [tip recordkeeping and reporting](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/tip-recordkeeping-and-reporting).

## What actually changes when a charge is wages instead of a tip?

Three separate things move at the same time, which is why one coding error produces several findings. Withholding, overtime math, and federal credit eligibility all shift together.

| | Tips | Mandatory service charges |
|---|---|---|
| Character | Tip income reported by the employee | Regular wages paid by the employer |
| Withholding | Based on reported tips | Standard wage withholding |
| Overtime | Outside the regular rate | Inside the regular rate, raising overtime |
| FICA tip credit | Eligible | Not eligible |

The overtime column is the one that quietly does the most damage. Service charge distributions enter the regular rate of pay, which raises the overtime rate for every hour above the threshold in that workweek. A payroll system told the money is a tip never performs that calculation. The federal framework for tipped pay and the regular rate sits in the [Wage and Hour Division fact sheet on tipped employees](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/15-tipped-employees-flsa).

The credit column costs money in the other direction. The federal credit applies to certain employee tips, and the mechanics are covered in [what is the FICA tip credit](/qa/what-is-the-fica-tip-credit). Payroll tax paid on service charge wages does not support the credit. A venue running mandatory charges through the tip line is overstating one thing and understating another simultaneously.

### Why a Nevada operator cannot reason from the wage rule alone

Nevada prohibits a tip credit against the minimum wage, and that rule is explained in [does Nevada allow a tip credit](/qa/does-nevada-allow-a-tip-credit). Some owners assume the state rule makes the tip versus service charge question irrelevant, since the wage floor has to be paid in cash either way.

The assumption does not hold. The Nevada rule controls the wage owed. The tip versus service charge distinction controls how additional money is characterized, how overtime is computed on it, and whether federal credit eligibility exists. Both rules apply, and they apply to different things.

Wage and hour treatment of a specific charge at a specific business is not a question this page can answer. The [Nevada Office of the Labor Commissioner](https://labor.nv.gov/About/Frequently_Asked_Questions/Frequently_Asked_Questions_-_About_Us/) handles state wage questions, and an employment attorney licensed in Nevada handles the rest. ## Where do Las Vegas businesses get service charge coding wrong most often?

In banquets and events, which is the heart of this market. Venues running weddings, conventions and large-party service push enormous volumes of mandatory charges, and the coding decision is usually made once by whoever set up the point of sale.

Consider the shape of the local economy. A catering company working the convention corridor may run three quarters of its revenue through contracts that carry a mandatory service charge. A hotel-adjacent banquet operation might invoice a hundred events in a heavy season, every one of them with a compulsory percentage on the contract. A steakhouse near the Strip applies an automatic gratuity on every party above a set size, which during a large trade show could be most tables on a Tuesday night.

Now repeat one misconfigured earnings code across that volume. The result is an overtime correction, an amended return, and an audit finding, all traceable to a single field nobody revisited.

### The seasonality multiplier

Convention weeks make everything harder at once. Staffing surges, temporary event workers appear on payroll for a handful of shifts, and administrative attention is at its lowest point precisely when transaction volume peaks.

Small operations feel this hardest. A twelve-person catering company staffing up to forty for a heavy show week is doing onboarding, scheduling and invoicing under pressure, and nobody in that building is auditing earnings codes on a Thursday in January.

The pattern repeats every year, which means an error introduced once compounds annually rather than resolving on its own.

## How should an operator fix the coding?

By classifying on the voluntariness test rather than the receipt label, and by keeping the two categories on separate earnings codes from the first day forward.

**Separate the codes.** Tips and service charges belong in distinct fields inside payroll, never combined into a single gratuity bucket for convenience.

**Verify the overtime feed.** Confirm that the service charge code actually feeds the regular rate calculation. Many systems allow the code to exist without the linkage, which looks correct on a wage statement and is not.

**Audit contract language against payroll treatment.** A banquet event order that states a required percentage is describing a service charge regardless of the noun used in the contract.

**Ask a tax professional about the credit base.** Removing service charges from the tip figure changes the credit calculation, and getting the base right matters more than getting the filing done fast.
