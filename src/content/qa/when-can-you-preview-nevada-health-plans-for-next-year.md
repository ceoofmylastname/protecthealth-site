The most useful weeks in Nevada's health insurance calendar are the ones almost nobody uses.

Open enrollment gets the advertising, the reminders, and the deadline panic. The preview period that runs just ahead of it gets silence, even though it offers the same plans and the same prices with none of the time pressure. Knowing when that window opens, and what it can and cannot do, is worth more than any amount of December hustle.

## When does the Nevada Health Link preview actually open?

Typically in October, in the weeks before open enrollment begins on November 1. Nevada Health Link announces the exact go-live date each fall rather than committing to a fixed date, so early October is when to start checking the exchange's site.

[Nevada Health Link](https://www.nevadahealthlink.com/) is the state's own marketplace, run by the Silver State Health Insurance Exchange on its own enrollment platform. When the preview goes live, the coming year's approved plans and rates are loaded into a browse-only tool: enter a ZIP code, the ages in the household, and an income estimate, and the tool returns the plans available in that county for the coming year with estimated premiums and estimated subsidy amounts.

Two boundaries define the preview. It shows next year's plans, not the current year's, so it answers the question that matters for open enrollment. And it is browse-only: no account, no application, no ability to enroll early. Enrollment itself waits for November 1.

## How does the preview fit the open enrollment calendar?

The preview runs ahead of an enrollment window that opens November 1 and closes January 15 for plan year 2027, with one mid-window date inside it that matters more than either endpoint for most households.

That date is December 31. Completing enrollment by December 31 generally produces coverage effective January 1, which means no gap for someone whose current plan ends in December and a clean handoff for someone switching plans. Enrollments completed between January 1 and January 15 generally take effect February 1 instead, leaving a one-month hole a switching household did not intend. The full calendar context sits in [when open enrollment is in Nevada](/qa/when-is-open-enrollment-in-nevada).

Read backwards, the calendar builds itself. Coverage January 1 requires enrolling by December 31. Enrolling well requires comparing plans, checking doctors, and estimating income, which takes a few evenings spread over weeks. Those weeks are exactly what the October preview provides, which is why the preview is less a curiosity than the actual start of the process. The federal marketplace runs a similar [preview tool](https://www.healthcare.gov/see-plans/) for healthcare.gov states, but Nevadans should use Nevada Health Link, where the state's real plans and rates live.

## What should a household do while the preview is open?

Three things: pull the plan list for the county, shortlist two or three candidates, and verify doctors and prescriptions against the shortlist. All three go faster with the household's lists already written down.

The comparison itself runs on five numbers per plan: premium after estimated subsidy, deductible, out-of-pocket maximum, network, and drug formulary, a method detailed in [what to compare when window shopping health plans](/qa/what-should-you-compare-when-window-shopping-health-plans). The verification step is the one that saves the most grief: every doctor the household intends to keep gets checked against each shortlisted plan's own directory, because Las Vegas networks shift between plan years and a plan that covered a doctor this year proves nothing about next.

Households already enrolled have one extra October task: read the renewal notice from the current plan, which lists what changes on January 1. Whether letting the current plan roll forward is safe, and what auto-renewal actually does, is the subject of [whether you can keep the same health plan next year](/qa/can-you-keep-the-same-health-plan-next-year).

One scheduling note for the households that need it most. Las Vegas hospitality workers heading into the fall convention season have less free time in November and December than in October, not more, which makes the preview weeks the realistic window for this work rather than merely the ideal one. An hour in October is available. The same hour in mid-December often is not.

## What are the limits of preview pricing?

Preview prices are estimates, and the estimate is only as good as the income projection entered. The final premium and subsidy get set during the real application, when household details and projected income are verified.

For salaried households, the preview estimate and the final number usually land close together. For the tipped, seasonal, and self-employed households that fill Clark County, the income projection is genuinely hard, and the preview is a good moment to take it seriously rather than guess. The subsidy is reconciled at tax time against actual income, so a careless estimate in October becomes a tax-season correction eighteen months later. The parent guide to [window shopping Nevada health plans](/blog-post/health-insurance-window-shopping-nevada) walks through building a defensible projection, and a household with truly tangled income should bring a licensed tax professional into that projection. We are insurance nerds, not tax professionals.

The preview asks for no documents, but the application will, and October is the cheap time to gather them: Social Security numbers, income records, and any immigration paperwork, per the checklist in [what documents you need for open enrollment](/qa/what-documents-do-you-need-for-open-enrollment).

A licensed Nevada broker can run the entire preview comparison with a household in one sitting, at no cost, and turn October browsing into a settled November decision. The way to claim one of those sittings is to [talk to a broker](/talk-to-a-broker) before the window opens and the calendar tightens.
