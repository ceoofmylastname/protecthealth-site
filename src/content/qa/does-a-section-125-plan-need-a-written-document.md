Of everything in this corner of employee benefits, this is the item most likely to be wrong in a small business right now, and the one with the largest downside when it is.

The pre-tax premium deduction on an employee's paystub is either supported by a written plan document or it is not supported by anything.

## Is a written document actually required?

Yes, and the requirement is specific about timing. A Section 125 cafeteria plan must be a separate written plan, adopted and effective on or before the first day of the plan year.

Retroactive adoption is not permitted. That single fact is what makes the gap unfixable after the fact: an employer who discovers in August that no document was ever adopted cannot produce one in August and cure January through July.

The plan document has to specify a description of each benefit offered and the period of coverage, the eligibility rules, the election procedures including the irrevocability rules and the periods during which elections are effective, how employer contributions may be made, the maximum contribution amount or the method for determining it, and the plan year.

## What happens without one?

The arrangement collapses entirely, and this is worth reading carefully because the failure is not proportional to the oversight.

If there is no written cafeteria plan, or the written plan fails the requirements, the arrangement is not a cafeteria plan and an employee's election between taxable and nontaxable benefits results in gross income to the employee. The same result follows if a plan fails to operate according to its written terms.

Follow the consequences through the payroll. Amounts every participant believed were pre-tax become taxable wages. Because those amounts are no longer excluded from the definition of wages, the employer loses the exclusion it has been relying on for Social Security, Medicare and federal unemployment tax, an exclusion confirmed on the FUTA side by the [Instructions for Form 940](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i940). What remains is underpaid payroll tax, W-2 corrections, and the penalty and interest that follow both.

### The scope is what makes it serious

Compare it to the other way a Section 125 plan can go wrong. A nondiscrimination failure taxes only the highly compensated individuals or key employees the plan favored, as [Publication 15-B](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p15b) describes.

A written plan failure has no such containment. There is no plan, so every participant is affected and the employer's own exclusion goes with it. One defect is a targeted correction. The other is a company-wide one.

## Why is this so often missing?

Because the payroll software makes it look done. This is the actual mechanism by which careful businesses end up out of compliance, and nobody involved is being careless.

An employer sets up health insurance, the payroll system offers a pre-tax deduction option, someone enables it because pre-tax is obviously better for the employee, and the deduction begins. Every paystub from that day forward looks completely normal. No error is generated, no notice arrives, and nothing in the system asks whether a plan document exists, because the software has no way to know.

Years pass. The arrangement is discovered during a benefits review, an acquisition, or an examination, by which point several plan years have run on a treatment nothing authorized.

Worth one note on authority. The detailed cafeteria plan regulations issued in 2007 remain proposed rather than final, and taxpayers generally rely on them. That is a reason to have the document drafted by a professional rather than assembled from a template, not a reason to treat the requirement as soft.

## What should an employer do about it?

Three checks, and the first one is a yes or no question somebody can answer today.

**Ask whether a written plan document exists, and get a copy.** Not a payroll screenshot and not a benefits summary. The plan document. If nobody can produce one, the pre-tax treatment currently being applied has nothing behind it.

**Check the plan year on the document against the plan year being run.** A document adopted years ago for a plan year that has since rolled over, or a document that names a plan year the business no longer follows, needs attention before the next year starts rather than after.

**Check who is listed as participating.** An excluded owner in the participant list is a separate defect, covered in [can an S corp owner participate in a Section 125 plan](/qa/can-an-s-corp-owner-participate-in-a-section-125-plan).

**Write down when the document was adopted, and keep the evidence.** The requirement is not merely that a document exists but that it was adopted and effective on or before the first day of the plan year. A signed and dated adoption record is what demonstrates that, and reconstructing it years later from memory is not the same thing. An employer whose document carries no adoption date has a document whose validity cannot be shown.
